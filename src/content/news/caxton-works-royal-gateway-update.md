## Latest update: 8 November 2026

### Council serves Dangerous Structure Notices on all five buildings

Newham Council has published details of the legal notices it has served on the five Royal Gateway buildings under the London Building Acts (Amendment) Act 1939. The council has formally decided that the buildings are dangerous structures, based on information from third parties, its own physical inspection and the necessary certification.

- **Centurion Tower, Sovereign Tower and St Vincent Court:** the council was alerted through a report to its Contact Centre at 12:30am on 23 October. Notices were served on 29 October.
- **Thanet Tower and Prince Court:** the council was made aware of concerns on 30 October. Notices were served on 5 November.

The notices were served on the owners of the buildings. **They have not been served on residents or businesses**, so you won't receive one and you don't need to do anything in response.

### What the owners are required to do

The notices set out what the council considers is needed to assess or remove the danger:

- immediate temporary works and risk mitigation
- structural monitoring, investigation and submission of information to the council
- a comprehensive structural appraisal and investigation of the materials
- a structural safety report and remedial works
- an independent external peer review
- continuing statutory responsibilities, with enforcement if they are not met
- general safety requirements, including restricting access to unsafe areas, putting temporary protective measures in place, and cooperating fully with Building Control officers during further inspections

The owners can formally dispute the certification or the actions required. If they neither dispute the notices nor comply with them, the council can take further enforcement action.

### What this means for residents

This puts the council's view that the buildings are dangerous on a legal footing, and it places clear duties on the owners to investigate and fix the problem, with independent checking. It is not the same as the prohibition notice that would trigger the council tax and business rates exemption, and Cllr Dasgupta and I are still pressing for that to be issued.

The notices do not set a date for anyone to return home. If you have questions about your situation, please [get in touch](mailto:John.Morris@newham.gov.uk).

## Update: 7 November 2026

### Galliard's letter on relocation costs and the road to getting residents home

Galliard Homes wrote directly to Royal Gateway residents on 5 November. I know many residents have received it, but I'm setting out what it says and what I'm still pushing for.

### Help with accommodation costs

- Galliard says it will reimburse the *reasonable* costs of relocation and temporary accommodation.
- For residents of Centurion Tower, Sovereign Tower and St Vincent Court, this runs from 23 October. For Thanet Tower and Prince Court, it runs from 30 October.
- This applies for an initial 14 days, up to and including Thursday 19 November. Galliard says it will write again before 19 November to confirm what happens after that.
- **Keep all your receipts and proof of payment.** Galliard says it will write with full details of how to claim and how payments will be made.
- Anything already paid or reimbursed through your own insurance, or from any other source, will be taken into account. You'll need to give details of any insurance claims when you submit costs.

### Getting you home

- Galliard says it is working with two engineering firms. Buro Happold is carrying out a detailed review of all five buildings, and Gravity Consulting Engineers is working on plans for temporary supports, permanent works, or both.
- Murphy Geospatial took over monitoring of the buildings on 2 November.
- Galliard has shared a proposed roadmap with the other parties involved. It covers monitoring, understanding the buildings, finding solutions, and independent review and approval before any works. Galliard says the first three stages will run at the same time where possible.
- The letter says it is too early to determine the extent or cause of the concerns, or where responsibility lies. **There is still no date for when residents can return.** Galliard says it will share a timetable "as soon as we can".

### Who to contact

Prime Property Management continues to manage the development for the Right to Manage companies. Contact Prime about building insurance, service charges, storage and access restrictions (contact details below).

### What I'm still pressing for

This letter is a step forward, but it leaves a lot unanswered. Alongside James Asser MP, Cllr Rohit Dasgupta and I are continuing to push for:

- a clear return timetable, and certainty on support beyond 19 November
- clarity on what counts as "reasonable" costs, and a simple, quick claims process so residents aren't left out of pocket
- action on mortgage payments, including lenders agreeing to pause them
- a freeze on council tax and business rates for affected residents and businesses
- an alternative venue for the nursery
- road closures around Caxton Works

If you are affected and have problems claiming costs or finding accommodation, please [get in touch](mailto:John.Morris@newham.gov.uk) and I will take your case up directly.

*Galliard states its support is provided without admission of liability and without prejudice to its position, including any rights against third parties.*

## Update: 5 November 2026

### Four businesses connected with new space

Cllr John Morris and Cllr Rohit Dasgupta have connected four affected businesses with Brunel Street Works and Hallsville Quarter, to help them find space to keep trading.

If your business has been affected and you would like help, please contact either councillor (details below).

### Pressing the council to issue a prohibition notice

We are pressing the council to issue a prohibition notice for the affected buildings. Once the notice is in place, council tax and business rates will be suspended for affected residents and businesses.

### James Asser MP writes to mortgage lenders

James Asser MP has written to mortgage lenders asking them to give affected borrowers a mortgage payment freeze. He has asked lenders to:

- offer an initial three-month payment holiday, extended if residents still cannot return home
- waive any associated fees
- make sure the arrangement does not harm borrowers' credit records
- set up a simple, fast-tracked process for residents of the five affected buildings

[Read James Asser MP's letter to mortgage lenders](/images/news/james-asser-mp-letter-mortgage-lenders-caxton-works.jpg)

If you have a mortgage on an affected property, contact your lender to ask what support it can offer.

## Update: 2 November 2026

### Council tax and business rates exemption agreed

Following our call for a freeze, the council's Head of Revenues and Finance Transactional Services has confirmed that the affected properties will be exempt from both council tax and business rates (NNDR). The exemption will apply once the council issues a prohibition notice, which legally prohibits the properties from being occupied. We are pressing the council to issue this notice.

### Help for nursery families

The council's Early Years team is contacting parents to help find alternative childcare places for children affected by the nursery's closure.

### Space for affected businesses

Your local councillors have contacted Brunel Street Works and Hallsville Quarter about space for businesses that have had to close. Brunel Street Works has confirmed it has units available.

If your business has been affected, please get in touch with Cllr John Morris or Cllr Rohit Dasgupta for further details:

## Original update: 31 October 2026

Prime Property Management has told residents of **Centurion Tower, St Vincent Court, Sovereign Tower, Thanet Tower and Prince Court** to leave their homes because of structural safety concerns. The first three blocks were evacuated on 23 October and the other two on 30 October. **Do not return to your home until further notice.**

Newham Council is supporting residents. For the latest official information, see the [council's page](https://www.newham.gov.uk/housing-homes-homelessness/centurion-tower-st-vincent-court-sovereign-tower).
