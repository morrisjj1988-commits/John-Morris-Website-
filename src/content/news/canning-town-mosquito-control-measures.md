Residents in Canning Town may have seen increased activity from council teams in recent weeks. This follows the identification of a small number of invasive mosquitoes in the area and a request from the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) to strengthen local control measures.

While the situation is being taken seriously, public health experts stress that the current risk to local residents remains very low. The enhanced response is a precautionary measure aimed at preventing the mosquitoes from becoming established in the area.

## What Has Been Found?

On 11 August 2026, a localised incident involving the invasive mosquito species Aedes aegypti was detected in a residential area of Canning Town through the UKHSA's Mosquito Watch surveillance programme. Since then, surveillance activities have identified 46 adult mosquitoes and several breeding sites within the affected area.

Aedes aegypti is a tropical mosquito species known internationally for its ability to transmit diseases such as dengue, chikungunya and Zika virus. However, there are currently no established populations of this mosquito in Northern Europe, and there is no indication of local disease transmission associated with this incident.

## Why Are Control Measures Being Expanded?

Although the overall health risk remains low, recent findings show ongoing breeding activity within the affected area. Additional larvae have been discovered, indicating that the mosquito population has not yet been eliminated. As a result, UKHSA has asked Newham Council to intensify control efforts.

The expanded programme will focus on approximately 330 properties located within a 300-metre radius of the incident. Council teams will be working with residents to identify and remove potential breeding sites, including containers that hold standing water. Drains may also be treated where appropriate, alongside enhanced waste and litter management measures.

## What Residents Can Do

Community participation is an important part of preventing mosquitoes from breeding.

Residents within the affected area are being encouraged to:

- Remove standing water from buckets, plant pots, watering cans and other outdoor containers.
- Refresh water in outdoor containers every few days.
- Check gardens and yards regularly for areas where rainwater may collect.
- Report mosquitoes and nuisance biting through the government's Mosquito Watch scheme.

Even small amounts of standing water can provide a breeding environment for mosquitoes, making these simple actions highly effective.

## How the Council Is Responding

Several council services are working together alongside UKHSA to manage the incident.

**Environmental Health**

Environmental Health officers have been visiting affected streets alongside mosquito surveillance specialists to provide guidance to residents and distribute information about mosquito control. Letters and information leaflets are also being sent to all properties within the affected area.

**Community Safety**

Community Safety teams are supporting local engagement activities and helping to distribute information to households.

**Parks and Open Spaces**

Additional litter collection is taking place in Keir Hardy Park, with particular attention being given to bushes and shrub areas where waste and water-holding items may accumulate.

**Street Scene Services**

Street cleaning and waste removal have also been increased within the response zone. More frequent litter picking and waste collection will help remove potential mosquito habitats and support wider environmental improvements.

## Working Together to Prevent Establishment

The objective of the current response is straightforward: eliminate breeding sites and prevent this invasive species from becoming established locally. UKHSA has also recommended involving the London Resilience Forum to support the wider multi-agency response.

While the discovery of an invasive mosquito species naturally attracts attention, experts emphasise that this is a highly localised incident and that control measures are already underway. By working together, local residents, council services and public health agencies can help ensure the mosquito population is removed and the area remains safe.

For further information on mosquito reporting, visit the government's Mosquito Watch service.
