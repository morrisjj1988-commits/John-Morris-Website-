Households across Newham will soon be able to get rid of old furniture, mattresses and appliances at no cost, as the council rolls out a new free bulky waste collection service from September.

## Three free collections a year

Under the scheme, every home in the borough will be entitled to three free bulky waste collections annually, with each visit covering up to six items — everything from televisions and tables to sofas and mattresses. Officials estimate the service could save residents up to £100 a year in disposal costs.

Mayor of Newham Forhad Hussain announced the return of the service, describing it as the fulfilment of a manifesto commitment.

## Tackling fly-tipping

The move is being framed as a central plank of the council's wider crackdown on fly-tipping, which officials say has become a persistent problem across the borough. The mayor said fly-tipping is a blight on the borough and that residents are tired of seeing waste dumped illegally on their streets.

The free collection service will sit alongside tougher enforcement measures. The council has already moved to double the maximum fine for fly-tipping from £500 to £1,000, and is expanding its use of CCTV to catch offenders in the act, with further measures planned.

## Reuse and recycling options

Alongside the new collection service, the council is encouraging residents to donate items that are still in good condition rather than throwing them away. Clean, usable furniture, white goods and bric-a-brac can be collected directly from homes for donation, with local options — including the TCL Reuse Centre — listed on the council's website.

Residents can also take items themselves to the Jenkins Lane Reuse and Recycling Centre, where furniture, bikes, clothing, paint and books can be reused, and other materials recycled.

## How to book

Households wishing to use the free bulky waste collection service will be able to book through the council once the scheme launches in September. Further details, including booking arrangements, are expected to be published on Newham Council's website ahead of the launch.
